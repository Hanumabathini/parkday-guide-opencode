# Research leads: similar national park guide sites

Found and checked Nov 15, 2026. Shared by the CEO (Hanuma) for all models — Muse, Claude, ChatGPT, and Grok when it returns. Research leads ONLY. Do NOT copy from them.

## Rules (CEO instruction)

- Never copy sentences, paragraphs, or text from any of these sites into our site, guides, or parks.json.
- Every fact taken from them (fee, permit, date, distance, season window) must be verified against NPS.gov or Recreation.gov BEFORE it enters parks.json or any guide. Rakesh fact-checks; nothing publishes unverified.
- Pictures may be copied if needed, only where we need a photo we do not already have; our own licensed photos come first; keep any credit line the site shows with the photo.
- Fees disagree between these sites (for example Everglades and Rocky Mountain are $35 on one site and $30 on another) — trust NPS.gov only.

## Key finding

Only 2 of the 11 sites already reflect 64 parks. All the rest still say 63, newest park New River Gorge (2020). Our scope is 64 parks, Chiricahua included — that alone sets us apart.

## The 11 sites

### 1. More Than Just Parks — morethanjustparks.com
63 on main list (its Arizona guide already counts Chiricahua as a 4th AZ park). Full guide per park, 1–5-day itineraries, drive-time tables, interactive map, rankings. Best for: guide articles, itineraries, parks.json fields (fees, visitation, acreage). Gaps: main list not yet 64; affiliate links; fees disagree with other sites.

### 2. Earth Trekkers — earthtrekkers.com
64 in index and best-time tables; authors visited 56 of 64. Month-by-month best-time guides for all 64, reservations guide, road-trip itineraries. Best for: best-months data for parks.json, itinerary and photography model. Gaps: Chiricahua has best-time data but no full guide yet; 8 parks not visited first-hand.

### 3. National Park Trekkers — nationalparktrekkers.com
64 ("64-park quest"). Only site found with a complete current Chiricahua guide (updated 2026-11-14): best months, weather and crowds by month, fees, hikes, 1–3-day itineraries, camping, lodging and gateway towns, RV limits, nearby parks. Best for: template for a 64-park park page; crowds/weather-by-month fields; the Chiricahua page itself. Gaps: heavy affiliate monetisation; newer site, less track record.

### 4. Best US National Parks — bestusnationalparks.com
63. Structured park pages: NPS alerts and news, campgrounds with prices, activities, points of interest, wildlife, weather, webcams, accessibility. Best for: parks.json schema model (structured fields per park); camping and activity data; planner filters. Gaps: still 63; visitor numbers inflated vs NPS 2025 (Smokies 12.9M shown vs 11.53M actual; Grand Canyon 6.4M vs 4.43M).

### 5. National Sites Guide — nationalsitesguide.com
Coverage unclear (organised by NPS records, no clean park count); Chiricahua still listed as a monument at its Oct 17, 2026 update. Yellowstone 1–5-day itinerary built strictly on the NPS drive-time table, with published methodology. Best for: itinerary-design methodology (drive-time-based, honest day limits). Gaps: thin on fees, trails, camping; little narrative.

### 6. Park'd — parkd.app
63. Checklist and map product: per-park quiet month, fee, visits, nearby parks; trip logging, stats and badges; 240 live webcams; "which park should I visit?" finder. Best for: trip-planner and checklist design ideas; quiet-month and cost fields. Gaps: still 63; best features in the mobile app, not the website; no deep per-park guides.

### 7. One Perfect Park Day — oneperfectparkday.com
63. One-day planner: per-park pages combine NPS alerts, facilities, weather, air quality and daylight into one-day plan cards with time, effort, confidence and a labelled source trail. Best for: one-day trip-planner design; accessibility-first planning; source-labelling model. Gaps: still 63; cached alerts looked stale (July warnings still showing in November); paid fieldbook upsell.

### 8. Visit US National Parks — visitusnationalparks.com
85 total sites, 63 pass-eligible (not parks-only — includes monuments and recreation areas). Fee, camping and seasons tables by region, with named campgrounds, site counts and reservation notes. Best for: fee and camping fields for parks.json. Gaps: not parks-only, rows need filtering to the 64 parks; fees disagree with other guides; little narrative or itinerary depth.

### 9. National Park Obsessed — nationalparkobsessed.com
63. Free printable 63-park checklist and map, most/least-visited lists, per-state best time and must-sees. Best for: checklist design and best-time/must-see starter fields. Gaps: the stalest site here — checklist text last updated Dec 2020, visitation figures from 2019; deeper guides are paid.

### 10. Best National Park — bestnationalpark.com
63. All 63 parks ranked across 7 criteria, top-10 for first-timers, best-by-interest lists. Best for: ranking and "best for…" editorial model; prioritising parks for first-time visitors. Gaps: factual slips spotted — Senior Pass called "$20 lifetime" (the $20 pass is annual), Smokies "12–14M/yr", duplicated entry in least-visited list; still 63.

### 11. AllTrails — alltrails.com (trails data only)
Trail database, not a park guide (for example 288 trails in Yellowstone). Each trail: difficulty, length, elevation gain, estimated time, ratings and photos. Best for: trails fields in parks.json (difficulty, distance, elevation, ratings). Gaps: no fees, permits, itineraries or camping; user-generated data can be stale on closures; offline maps behind paywall.

### Also noted, not fully assessed
IASNOVA interactive 64-park atlas (iasnova.com/interactive-atlas/us-national-parks-map/) — covers all 64 including Chiricahua with size and visits per park; a visual atlas rather than a guide; may be worth a look for map design.
