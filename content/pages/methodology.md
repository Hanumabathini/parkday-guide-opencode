Every published fact on Parkday Guide is meant to be traceable to a primary source. In practice that means NPS.gov for park information and Recreation.gov for reservations and booking related details. When you read a statement about a park or a gateway town, you should be able to find where it came from and when we last checked it.

## Where facts come from

We use official sources first. For a park fact, the reference point is the National Park Service site. For a reservation or booking fact, the reference point is Recreation.gov. We do not use other guides as a source of facts, and we do not copy their wording. If a detail cannot be confirmed against a primary source, we leave it out or mark it clearly as unverified rather than filling the gap with a guess.

## Retrieval dates

A fact without a date is hard to trust. Each item we publish carries a retrieval date, the day we looked at the source and confirmed the statement. This date tells you how fresh the information is. It does not tell you that the source has stayed unchanged since. Before you rely on a detail for a decision, use the source link to check the current version. This is especially true for anything connected to access, availability, or reservations.

## Verification labels

Pages and facts carry labels that describe their state, not their quality. A verified item is one we have checked against a primary source and recorded a retrieval date for. A pending verification label means the fact is drafted but the check is not finished, so you should treat it as provisional. A needs recheck label means our recorded date is old enough that we want to confirm the item again. These labels are deliberately plain. They are a note to you about how much work has gone into a page, and they change as we finish the checks.

## Rechecking

We recheck facts on a schedule and whenever a source signals a change. Rechecking is not a single pass. We look for the same statement in the current source, compare it with what we published, and update the retrieval date when the statement still holds. If the source now says something different, we change the text to match the source rather than keeping the older version and adding a footnote.

## Corrections

If you find something that looks wrong, send the page, the exact statement, and the source you believe is correct. We review corrections against the primary source and update the text and retrieval date if the source supports the change. We do not treat a correction as settled until it has been checked. Corrections are part of the build, not an exception to it, and the accessibility page explains how to send one about access information specifically.
