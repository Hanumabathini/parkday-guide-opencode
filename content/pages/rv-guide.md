An RV trip around national parks asks for a different kind of planning than a trip where you carry everything in a car. You are managing a place to sleep, a place to park, and a supply chain at the same time. This guide keeps the advice general on purpose. Park rules, road limits, and site dimensions vary, so check the current official source for the place you are visiting.

## Start with the shape of the trip

Decide first whether you are using one park as a base or linking several parks and towns. A single base keeps logistics simple and lets you return to the same setup each evening. A route with several stops gives you more variety but asks more of your planning, because each new overnight needs its own confirmation. Write down the order of your stops before you look for individual sites. That order determines where you need fuel, water, and a place to stay.

## Confirm before you commit

For any overnight or parking decision, go to the primary source. Recreation.gov is the reference for reservable sites, and NPS.gov is the reference for park rules and road information. Check the current wording rather than relying on a summary, including a summary in this guide. Confirm any statement about vehicle length, road suitability, or where camping is allowed against the source, and record when you checked it. If the official source does not address a question, treat that as unknown and plan a fallback.

## Plan for resupply around park days

RV trips tend to fail at the margins, not in the middle of a park day. Work out where you can refill water, dispose of waste, and buy food before you arrive. Gateway towns are useful here, because they are usually the places between park days where you can reset. Build a route that gives you a resupply option before your tanks or supplies run low, and keep one extra option in case your first choice is unavailable.

## Keep the plan adjustable

Weather, road conditions, and availability can change a route. Keep your itinerary in a form you can edit, and separate the parts that are fixed from the parts you can move. A confirmed overnight is fixed. A preferred stop with no confirmation is flexible. When you read a park page on this site, use the verification label and retrieval date to judge how much weight to give a detail, then confirm the current source before you build a plan around it.
