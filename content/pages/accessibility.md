Accessibility is a practical concern for this guide, not a badge. Our intent is to describe access in terms you can act on, and to be clear about the limits of what we currently document. We are not claiming that the site conforms to WCAG or any other standard. The build is still in progress, and accessibility work is part of that work rather than something finished and set aside.

## What we aim to do

Access details change with seasons, shuttles and trail work, and they vary from one route to the next. We do not yet maintain a verified access dataset for every park. Where a page touches on access, it points to the official source rather than restating a claim we have not checked.

## What is documented per park

At launch, each park page carries official fees, hours, contacts, activities and a link to the park's own page, where the National Park Service publishes its accessibility information. The service also gathers its accessibility guidance at [nps.gov/subjects/accessibility](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/accessibility/index.htm). We do not claim that every facility, trail or route is described here. Where the official source is silent, we leave the detail out rather than guess.

Several gaps remain during the pre-launch build. We do not provide a complete picture of surface conditions, gradients, or the day to day state of every route. We do not present photo based route assessments, and our photography comes from the National Park Service with credit, or from commissioned work credited to the photographer. Treat the guide as a starting point for questions, not the final answer about whether a specific route will work for you.

## Requesting a correction

If you find an access detail that is wrong or missing, send the page title, the exact statement, and the primary source you are using. If the source is not public, describe what it says in enough detail that we can look for an equivalent official reference. We review each request against the primary source before changing the text, and we update the retrieval date when we make a change. Corrections about access are handled with the same care as any other fact, and we would rather hear about a gap than leave it unresolved.
