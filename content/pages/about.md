Parkday Guide is a planning resource for people who want to spend time in the United States national park system without piecing together a trip from a dozen disconnected sources. It is being built from scratch, one park and one gateway town at a time, with a defined scope of 64 national parks. This is a pre-launch build; the launch home will be parkdayguide.com.

## Who this is for

You are planning a trip and you want clear, checkable information in one place. Perhaps you are comparing two parks and trying to understand what each area is like before you commit. Perhaps you have already chosen a park and you want to know what the nearby towns offer, how to prepare for the day, and what to do once the visit is over. This guide is written for both situations. It assumes no prior expertise and it does not assume you have unlimited time or a fixed route.

## What it covers

Each park entry is organised around three phases. Planning covers the decisions you make before you travel, including how to read official park information and what to confirm for yourself. The visit covers the on-the-ground day, with attention to how you move through a park and how you treat the places you pass through. After the park covers the town and the surrounding area, so the end of a park day does not mean the end of a useful plan.

Alongside the park pages, the guide documents gateway towns, the places people usually stay, eat, and resupply between park days. The intent is a single starting point that carries you from the first question through the last evening of a trip. You should not have to keep a separate list of tabs just to answer basic questions.

## How to read the guide

This is a pre-launch build. Every park and gateway town in scope has a page, and the official facts on those pages carry a retrieval date and a verification label. A label describes the state of the check behind a statement, not the quality of the park. You can read the methodology page for the full explanation of how facts are sourced and dated.

We publish nothing that we have not traced to NPS.gov or Recreation.gov, and we record when each official fact was retrieved. Editorial is written from scratch and checked against the park's official page before release. If a detail matters to your decision, follow the source link and confirm it yourself before you travel. Rules and availability change, and a guide can only tell you what was true when the fact was checked.

Photography on the site comes from the National Park Service with credit, or from commissioned work credited to the photographer. You will not find lifted images presented as a park scene. The goal is a record that is useful, modest, and honest about what it knows and what it does not yet know.
