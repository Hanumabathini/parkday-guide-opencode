Parkday Guide exists because planning a national park trip tends to be scattered across too many places. You start with an official park page, move to a booking site, then hunt for something about the nearby town, and finish with a list of saved links you no longer trust. The information is not necessarily wrong. It is just split into pieces that do not fit together well when you are trying to make a decision.

## A single path through the trip

We wanted one place that follows the same question from start to finish. What should I know before I go? What does the day in the park actually involve? What happens when the park day ends and I am in a gateway town with the rest of the evening ahead? Those three questions sit behind the structure of the site. Planning, visit, and after are not sections added for neatness. They are the order in which you make decisions.

## A defined scope

The guide covers 64 national parks and their gateway towns. A fixed scope keeps the work honest. It tells you what is in and what is not, and it lets us finish a park properly before moving to the next one. We would rather have a well checked page for one park than a shallow page for every park. Every park and town in scope now has a page, and the pre-launch work is about rechecking and improving what is there.

## Facts you can trace

The other reason for building from scratch is trust. Every published fact is meant to be checked against NPS.gov or Recreation.gov, with a retrieval date recorded. That discipline shapes what we write. If we cannot confirm a detail, we leave it out or say plainly that it is unverified. You will not find copied wording from other sites, and you will not find invented prices, hours, or policies presented as fact.

## Who benefits

This is for the person who wants to be prepared without becoming a researcher. You should be able to open a park page, understand the current state of the information, and see where to confirm anything that matters. The photography is ground and local photography credited to the photographer, so the images show the place rather than a staging of it. The site is still being built, and the free 64 Parks Planning Checklist is one way we help you carry the plan off the screen. If that sounds like the kind of guide you want, the methodology page explains how the verification works.
