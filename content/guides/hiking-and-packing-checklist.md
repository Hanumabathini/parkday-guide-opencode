Most hiking problems come from small omissions rather than bad decisions on the trail. A forgotten layer, an empty bottle, or a phone with no signal turns a short walk into a long afternoon. Pack from a list, then adjust it for the park's own conditions.

## Check the park's guidance before you pack

Every park publishes current information about weather, wildlife, water sources, and trail status, and it changes with the season. Read it before you decide what to carry. A trail that is straightforward in one month can be snow-covered or flooded in another.

Note where water is actually available. If there is none along your route, your pack weight depends on how much you carry, which changes how far you should plan to go.

## The core list for any day hike

- A charged phone with offline maps, plus a paper map and a compass you know how to use
- More water than you expect to drink, and a way to treat or refill it if sources exist
- Food that needs no preparation, plus a little extra for a delayed return
- Layers for sun, wind, and cold, packed so you can reach them without stopping
- Sun protection, a headlamp, and a small first-aid kit
- A whistle, a knife or tool, and any medication you take daily
- Cash and identification in a waterproof bag

Everything on that list earns its place by covering a problem you cannot predict in advance.

## Set rules for the day before you start

Agree on a turnaround time and a point where you turn back if the weather changes. Tell someone your route and when you expect to be back. Start early enough that you are not rushing the return, and keep the pace set by the slowest person in the group.

Wildlife guidance differs by park and season, so read it and follow the distances and storage rules the park sets. If a trail is closed, do not work around the barrier.

## Rehearse the list at home

Lay everything out before you travel and pack it once. You will notice duplicates, missing items, and things you never use, and you will have time to replace them. This also reveals how heavy the pack actually is, which matters more than you think when the ground is uneven.

Keep the items you need in an emergency in the same place every time, so you are not searching at the end of a long day.

## Keep the pack light enough to use

Carry only what you will actually reach for, and place the items you need most often at the top. A heavy pack changes your balance on rough ground and makes every stop slower. Test your loaded pack at home before you travel, and adjust it there rather than on the trail.

### Verify before you go
- Read current trail, weather, and water guidance on [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov).
- Check permit and shuttle requirements for your route on [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov).
- Confirm the trail status and any closures the day before you hike.
- Pack the core list, then remove anything you will not use.
- Set a turnaround time, share your plan, and carry your map offline and on paper.
- Pack at home first, and remove anything you will not reach for on the trail.
