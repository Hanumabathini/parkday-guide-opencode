The trip does not end at the gate, because the last hour of a visit decides how easy the next one is. A short routine for gear, notes, and feedback keeps your equipment usable and your future planning honest. It also takes only a few minutes.

## Handle waste and gear before you unpack

Separate anything wet from the rest of your kit, and air out tents, boots, and sleeping bags before you store them. Damp gear packed away starts to smell and degrades faster, so a few hours of drying saves a replacement later.

Clean out food wrappers, crumbs, and coolers, and check pockets for tickets, permits, or receipts you may need. Note anything that failed, broke, or ran out, while you still remember exactly what happened.

## Write down what actually worked

Jot a few lines while the details are fresh: which days felt too full, which stop was worth the time, what you carried but never used, and what you wished you had. Future you will not remember the details, and this short note is worth more than any generic advice.

Record the practical parts too. Which entrance was easier, how early you needed to leave, where fuel and food were reliable, and what you would base yourself near next time. Patterns show up across two or three trips and make each one smoother.

## Close the loop on bookings and belongings

Cancel or use any remaining reservations properly, return rented or borrowed equipment, and check that nothing left behind is still reserved under your name. If you bought an annual pass, keep it somewhere you will find it before the next trip.

Report anything broken, unsafe, or wrongly marked while you are still able to describe it accurately. Feedback given quickly is far more useful than feedback given months later.

## Keep the useful paper and delete the rest

Permits, confirmations, and passes are worth keeping until you are sure you are finished with them, especially if a refund or a claim might follow. Store them in one place rather than scattered across a camera roll and an inbox. Everything else, brochures and maps included, can be recycled once your notes are written.

If you recorded photographs or notes about conditions you met, keep them with your trip record. Weather, closures, and trail state at the time of your visit give you a realistic picture for comparing future seasons.

## Start the next plan with better questions

With your notes in hand, the next planning session gets easier. You will know whether you prefer one park or several, whether in-park lodging or an outside base suits you, and how much slack your days need. Those answers matter more than any list of places to go.

### Verify before you go
- Recheck any outstanding bookings and passes on [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov).
- Read the park's current guidance on waste, food storage, and reporting on [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov).
- Confirm that everything you packed out is packed out, and nothing wet is stored away.
- Save your trip notes somewhere you will find them when planning the next visit.
- Note the one change you would make next time, and keep it short enough to act on.
- Store permits and confirmations in one place until any refund or claim is settled.
