Many parks now ask visitors to reserve a slot before arriving, especially during busy seasons. These systems are not uniform, and a rule that applied one year may not apply the next. The safest approach is to treat every reservation detail as provisional and confirm it on the official park page.

## Know what you are reserving for

There are several distinct things you might need a reservation for. Entry reservations control access to the park on a given day or during a given window. Timed entry controls when you may pass a specific entrance or corridor. Permits cover activities such as overnight stays in backcountry areas, lotteries for popular routes, or limited-capacity experiences.

Each has its own booking system and its own release schedule. Read which one applies to the areas you actually plan to visit, because booking the wrong thing is a common and avoidable mistake.

## Book in the order that makes sense

Start with the hardest constraint first. If you need a permit to enter a specific area, secure that before you commit to lodging, because the rest of the trip only works if the permit exists. If entry is the constraint, confirm it before you book flights or long drives.

Release dates and booking windows change, and some are announced only a short time ahead. Check the park's own page for how and when releases happen rather than relying on a schedule you saw somewhere else.

## If the slot is unavailable, work outward

A full booking does not end the trip. Options usually include shifting to a different day, entering at a different entrance, visiting outside the busiest window, or basing yourself outside the park and coming in for shorter visits. Some parks release cancellations close to the date, so a full calendar is not necessarily a closed one.

Ask what the park allows without a reservation, since some areas remain open under normal entry rules even when a timed system is in place elsewhere.

## Read what happens if plans change

Cancellation rules, no-show penalties, and transfer options differ between systems, and knowing them protects you from losing a booking you could have moved. Check how late you can change a date, whether a slot can be passed to someone else, and what happens if the park closes the area on your day.

If the park restricts entry because of weather or an incident, the official channels will say so. Keep them handy rather than relying on a screenshot from months ago.

## Keep proof and a backup plan

Save confirmations somewhere that works offline, note the exact entry window, and carry identification that matches the booking. Plans move, so write down what you will do if weather, road work, or a closure invalidates your slot.

### Verify before you go
- Confirm current reservation and timed-entry rules on [nps.gov](https://www.nps.gov).
- Book camping, permits, and lotteries through [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov).
- Read the park page for release dates, entry windows, and what happens if a road closes.
- Save confirmations offline and note the exact date, window, and entrance on your booking.
- Decide your backup plan now, including where you will go if your slot cannot be used.
- Note the cancellation deadline and whether your booking can be changed or transferred.
